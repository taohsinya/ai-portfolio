# Kikitrade: "Never Trade Crypto Alone"

**Role:** Co-founder & CEO · 2020–2023 · Hong Kong, Taiwan and Southeast Asia

## The problem

By 2020 there were already many places to buy crypto. A new app could not win by being "another exchange". We needed a reason for people to choose us, and a reason to stay.

## What I did

**1. Found a niche the big players were not serving.**
Two things together:
- **Deposits in Hong Kong dollars.** People in Hong Kong could put money in with their own currency, simply.
- **Social trading.** Users could follow other traders, see what they were doing and talk about it in the app.

Kikitrade became **Hong Kong's first crypto social trading platform**.

**2. Put the idea into one line.**
The slogan was **"Never Trade Crypto Alone."** It told people what was different in four words: here you trade with a community, not by yourself.

**3. Used people, not just ads, to spread the word.**
A social product should grow through people. We worked closely with **500+ KOLs** (key opinion leaders, or online influencers) across Hong Kong and Taiwan. They brought their own followers into the app, and those followers made the community feel alive for the next user.

**4. Watched a small set of numbers.**
- Cost per install (CPI): what it costs to get one person to download the app.
- Funded conversion: the share of users who actually put money in.

## Results

- **200,000+ users.**
- Cost per install of about **$3.5**.
- About **5%** of users went on to fund their accounts.
- About **$4M** in cumulative revenue.
- At its peak, about **$80M** in assets on the platform. At the time it was the largest platform in Hong Kong for buying crypto with Hong Kong dollars, and the only one with social features.

## What I learned

- **A niche is a strategy, not a weakness.** "Hong Kong dollars + social" was small enough to own and clear enough to explain.
- **A slogan should carry the product.** If the line explains why you are different, every ad, post and KOL video gets easier to make.
- **For a social product, the community is the marketing.** KOLs did more than send traffic. They gave new users someone to follow on day one.
