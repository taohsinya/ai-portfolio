# My method: getting cited by AI, with SEO as the foundation

**What I do: help companies show up faster in Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT and other AI assistants.**

How I get a product **cited in AI answers** such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews (**GEO**), and found on Google (**SEO**), from a brand-new site upwards. It combines what I did in practice with current published research (sources at the end).

## Why AI citations now come first

More and more people ask an AI assistant instead of clicking through ten blue links. When the AI answers, it names a few sources, and those few get the trust and the visit. So the goal has shifted:

| | Old goal | Goal now |
|---|---|---|
| What you win | A high position on Google | Being **named and linked** in the AI's answer |
| What proves it | Rankings, site authority | **How often AI assistants cite or mention you**, and your share of mentions against competitors |
| What drives it | Links and keywords | Clear, sourced, quotable answers, plus being discussed on the sites AI trusts |

SEO still matters: AI answers lean on pages that search engines already find, index and trust. It's the foundation, no longer the finish line.

```
1. Map intent  →  2. Study competitors  →  3. Pick the fight your site can win
        →  4. Write pages AI can quote  →  5. Build authority off-site
        →  6. Make sure Google can index it  →  7. Measure, then repeat
```

---

## 1. Map intent before volume

Sort searches by **how close the person is to a decision**, not by how many people search:

| Stage | What they type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | "why do I always feel broke" | Builds awareness |
| Options | "how to set financial goals" | Builds trust |
| Comparison | "is a financial advisor worth it" | **High** |
| Decision | "best tool to check if I'm on track to retire" | **Highest** |

Start with the comparison and decision stages. A small search from someone about to decide is worth more than a big search from someone browsing. Money topics are what Google calls *"Your Money or Your Life"*: it holds them to a higher standard, so trust matters even more than usual (see step 4).

## 2. Study what already works for competitors

In Ahrefs, look at competitors' **top pages** and the **root keywords** (the core words a whole group of searches is built around) that bring them most traffic. Then look for:
- **Gaps:** questions they rank for that you don't cover
- **Pages that spread:** the ones that earned many links or social shares, which show what people want to share
- **Weak spots:** high-intent searches where the current top results are thin or out of date

## 3. Pick the fight your site can win

What decides this is your site's **authority** (Ahrefs *Domain Rating*, DR), not the size of the company:

| Your site | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New or weak (DR under ~20) | Long-tail phrases of 3–5 words with low difficulty (KD under ~20), plus free tools such as calculators | Easier to rank, clearer intent, early wins build authority |
| Growing (DR ~20–50) | A mix: keep the long tail and start on mid-difficulty terms | Authority starts to carry |
| Established (DR 50+) | Compete directly on root keywords | You can now win the big terms |

Group pages into **clusters**: one main "pillar" page on a broad topic, linked to many "spoke" pages answering specific questions. Each ranking page lifts the others.

## 4. Write pages that AI can quote

AI assistants pull short, self-contained passages they can trust. Research from Princeton found that adding **sources, statistics and quotations** raised visibility in AI answers by about 30–40%. In practice:
- **Answer first.** Put a 1–2 sentence answer directly under each heading.
- **Back every number with a source**, and link to it.
- **Add a FAQ** with real questions people ask.
- **Use structured data** (Article, FAQ, Organization) so machines can read the page.
- **Show who is behind it**: author, review process and date updated. For money topics this is not optional.
- **Write natively in each language**; don't translate word for word.

## 5. Build authority off-site (the 0 → 1 phase)

A new site has no reputation, so it has to borrow one.

**Links that pass ranking strength ("do-follow"):**
- Guest posts on relevant sites
- Original data or research that others want to cite (for example, a benchmark testing how well AI tools answer money questions)
- Partnerships and press

**Links and mentions that don't pass ranking strength directly ("no-follow"), but still matter:**
- **Reddit, YouTube, X (Twitter), Google Images, social recommendations.** They bring real visitors, help Google discover new pages faster, and teach AI systems that your brand exists and what it's about. AI answers often draw on exactly these places, which makes this a core part of GEO.

**One rule:** never buy links on money topics. If Google catches it, the penalty costs more than the links were worth.

## 6. Make sure Google can index it

Publishing isn't the same as being found. Before blaming content, check:
- A **sitemap for each language** is in the right place
- Pages **link to each other** (internal links)
- Each page says **something different** from your other pages and from the rest of the web
- After a fix, **wait for Google to re-crawl** (often weeks) before judging the result

## 7. Measure, then repeat

| Tool | What I watch |
|---|---|
| **Ahrefs Brand Radar** | How often ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and AI Overviews mention or cite us, and our share against competitors |
| **Google Search Console** | Impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode; which searches show our pages; which pages are indexed |
| **Bing Webmaster Tools** | Which of our pages Microsoft's AI cites, and for which questions |
| **Google Analytics** | Visitors arriving from AI tools and from search, and what they do: sign-ups, not just visits |
| **AITDK** | Quick checks on AI-search visibility and page-level SEO |
| **Ahrefs Site Explorer** | Referring domains and keywords: the foundation behind the citations |

Split the numbers into two kinds:
- **Early signals** (weeks): pages indexed, new referring domains, first mentions in AI answers
- **Results** (months): **AI citations and share of mentions**, visits from AI tools and search, sign-ups and revenue

Early signals tell you whether you're on track long before results arrive. Both SEO and AI citations compound, so the first months mostly build the base.

## Where I've applied it

- **An AI investing app I co-founded:** built its search presence from a brand-new domain.
- **AI citations:** worked to get the product named in AI assistants' answers to everyday money questions, through quotable, sourced articles in English and Chinese, and a published study testing how well AI tools answer money questions.
- **BlockTempo, a Taiwan crypto media brand:** coached its team hands-on on SEO strategy as part of the venture studio's support.
- **A rebrand onto a new domain:** the hard lesson that authority belongs to the domain. When a product moves to a new web address, it starts again from zero unless the old address is properly redirected to the new one.
