# Digital marketing: from idea to tested ad, fast

**What I do:** turn a product idea into ad-ready creative quickly, test it on YouTube, Meta and TikTok, and put the money behind what works. AI makes the production step cheap ([see AI video ads](../ai-video/)), so the real skill is choosing **what to say** and **finding out fast whether it works**.

```
1. Study the best ads in the category
   → 2. Find the selling point, the audience and the 3-second punchline
   → 3. Write it as a brief with many hooks
   → 4. Spin one idea into many themes and formats
   → 5. Test on YouTube, Meta and TikTok; scale winners, kill losers
   → back to 1
```

---

## 1. Study the best ads in the category

Before making anything, find out what already works for competitors.

**Where I look:**
- **Meta Ad Library** (free): every ad a company is running on Facebook and Instagram
- **Foreplay**: saves ads from Meta, TikTok and LinkedIn into an organised swipe file
- **GetHookd**: a large library of ads and hooks, searchable by niche
- **TikTok Creative Center**: top-performing ads and trends on TikTok
- A folder of competitors' videos downloaded and broken down frame by frame

**One rule:** only study ads that have been **running for three months or more**. Nobody keeps paying for an ad that loses money, so a long-running ad is close to proof that it works.

**For each strong ad, I write down:**

| Question | Example answers |
|---|---|
| What is it selling? (the angle) | Save money, feel in control, beat the market, don't be alone |
| What format? | Podcast clip, stand-up comedy, street interview, creator talking to camera, short skit |
| What happens in the first 3 seconds? | A surprising number, a confession, a contradiction, a direct challenge |
| What's the offer and the call to action? | Free report, free check-up, trial |

The goal isn't to copy. It's to see which angles and formats the market already rewards, and where nobody is talking yet.

## 2. Find the selling point, the audience and the 3-second punchline

Three questions decide everything that follows:
1. **What is the one reason people choose us?** (the selling point)
2. **Who exactly is it for?** (the audience)
3. **What can we say in the first 3 seconds that makes that person stop scrolling?** (the punchline)

Two examples from paid campaigns I ran:

**Uplive (live-streaming app, 50+ countries).** The selling point in Asia was **company during bored, empty moments, and meeting streamers from other places**: viewers in mainland China watching streamers in Taiwan, viewers in Saudi Arabia watching streamers in Morocco. The idea stayed the same everywhere, but each market got its own creative built around its own culture, humour and faces.

**An AI investing app.** We attracted users with things they wanted right away: **stock lists and free reports**. The free value was the ad; the product was what they found when they came for it.

## 3. Write it as a brief with many hooks

Turn the selling point into a one-page brief: audience, the feeling we want, the one message, the proof, the offer. Then write **many hooks for the same message**. The hook decides most of an ad's result: if the first 3 seconds fail, nobody sees the rest.

An **audience × hook grid** keeps this organised: each row is a type of user, each column a type of hook (surprising number, confession, contradiction, challenge, hard lesson). Every cell is a possible ad.

## 4. Spin one idea into many themes and formats

One proven selling point should become dozens of ads, not one:
- **Themes:** different everyday situations where the same problem shows up
- **Formats:** podcast clip, stand-up, street interview, skit
- **Markets:** the same idea in each market's own culture and language (as with Uplive)
- **Sizes:** vertical for TikTok and Reels, square and landscape for feeds and YouTube

This is where AI pays off. Our pipeline turned **one idea into 42 versions** (14 themes × 3 sizes), and a strict AI scorer killed weak scripts before any money was spent. Details: [AI video ads](../ai-video/).

## 5. Test on YouTube, Meta and TikTok; scale winners, kill losers

**Test the hook first.** Keep the rest of the ad the same and try 3–5 different openings. Hooks are cheap to change and matter most, so they're tested before the body or the offer.

**What I watch, in order:**

| Stage | Number | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | Share of viewers who watch past 3 seconds | Does the hook work? |
| Hold | How long people keep watching | Does the story keep them? |
| Click | Click-through rate | Does the offer appeal? |
| Convert | Cost per install, cost per sign-up, return on ad spend | Does it make money? |

**Each platform rewards something different:** TikTok likes raw, creator-style energy with sound on; Meta tolerates more polish; YouTube Shorts rewards stories people watch to the end. The same idea usually needs a different cut for each.

**Decide in advance** what counts as a winner (move more budget to it and make variations) and what counts as a loser (stop it and move on). Then check where the click lands: a great ad pointing at a vague landing page wastes the budget.

## A new channel: ads inside ChatGPT

ChatGPT now sells ads, and I've tested it as a channel. **So far, conversion hasn't been strong enough** to move budget from YouTube, Meta and TikTok. That matches early market data: click-through rates are far below Google Search, results look better on very specific, high-intent questions, and reporting is still basic. I treat it as a channel to keep testing in small amounts, especially for questions where someone is close to a decision.

## When you can't buy ads

Paid ads aren't always allowed. Crypto products, for example, are heavily restricted on the big ad platforms. At **Kikitrade** (crypto social trading, Hong Kong) we grew without them, using three channels:

1. **Referrals through KOLs.** We worked with many KOLs (online influencers) in Hong Kong and Taiwan. When someone they referred signed up and became an active user, we paid a reward.
2. **Deal and coupon sites.** We partnered with Hong Kong "jetso" platforms (sites people visit for deals and giveaways) and promoted Kikitrade with sign-up codes.
3. **Free mini-classes with KOLs.** We ran podcast-style classes with KOLs, then brought the listeners into our community and kept talking with them on social media.

The insight behind it: what **brought people in** was depositing and withdrawing easily in Hong Kong dollars; what **kept them** was not feeling alone when prices swung. That became the brand line, *"Never Trade Crypto Alone"*, and the community became the product.

At **OpenSocial** (a social app platform) we didn't advertise the platform at all. Three apps built on it, **SoMon, BreadnButter and Zeek**, pulled users in by combining games, social features and sharing, so every player who invited a friend brought another user onto the platform.

## Results

| Campaign | Channels | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Uplive | Paid ads in many markets, plus TV, metro and bus ads | **30M users in 50+ countries** in three years; ~US$10M yearly budget at up to **150% return on ad spend** |
| Kikitrade | No paid ads: KOL referrals, deal sites, mini-classes, community | **200,000+ users**, about **US$3.5 per install**, **5%** of users funded their accounts, about **US$4M** cumulative revenue |
| An AI investing app | Paid video ads, free stock lists and reports | **480,000 users** and **US$1M** in annual recurring revenue |

## What I've learned

- **The selling point matters more than production.** AI made video cheap; it didn't make good ideas cheap.
- **What brings people in and what keeps them are often different.** At Kikitrade, easy deposits brought users in; not feeling alone kept them.
- **No ads allowed doesn't mean no growth.** Referrals, partners and community can do the job when paid channels are closed.
- **Localise the idea, not just the language.** The same selling point needs different faces, humour and situations in each market.
- **Let something people already want do the advertising**: a game, a free report, a stock list.
- **Long-running competitor ads are free research.** Someone else has already paid to find out what works.
